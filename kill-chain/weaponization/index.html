Understanding Weaponization

Weaponization is the preparation or adaptation of a capability for use against the target. In the classic model, this often means combining an exploit with a payload and packaging it into a deliverable artifact. The capability may be developed, purchased, reused, or modified; it does not have to be novel.

Typical activities

  • Select or adapt an exploit and the payload intended to run after access.
  • Prepare a malicious document, executable, script, or modified software artifact for a chosen delivery route.
  • Configure payload behavior and use packaging or obfuscation to make the artifact usable in the intended environment.

Example: An attacker prepares a document containing malicious content before sending it. Preparing that artifact is weaponization; sending it is delivery; triggering its behavior belongs to the later execution or exploitation analysis.

Much of this work happens outside the defender’s environment. Recovered samples, build artifacts, and intelligence can reveal preparation, but imports, strings, and file structure indicate possible capabilities rather than proof that they ran.

Defender’s question: What was the artifact prepared to do, and which independent evidence would confirm those capabilities?

What to look for

Study payload structure and provenance. Static capability clues support hypotheses; they do not prove execution.

Use the model with care

The classic seven stages of Lockheed Martin’s Cyber Kill Chain are an organizing model, not a mandatory linear sequence. Stages can repeat, overlap, be skipped, or remain unobserved. Fileless, insider, identity, cloud, and AI attacks may not fit every stage. A reading association is not proof that an observed attack completed that stage.

MITRE ATT&CK describes adversary behaviors and tactical goals at a different level of detail. Its tactics are not interchangeable with these stages. The external technique links below are learning associations, not an official one-to-one mapping.

Start with CTI Kill Chain: An Analyst Guide With Real-World Evidence for case evidence and analytic limits, or the original framework reference (PDF).

Methods across stages

Use the CTI Analyst Field Manual for evidence and confidence, detection engineering guide for telemetry and validation, and practical learning paths for bounded exercises. These methods support several stages; they are not additional Kill Chain stages.

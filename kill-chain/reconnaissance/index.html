Understanding Reconnaissance

Reconnaissance is the preparation in which an adversary learns enough about a target to choose an approach. The information may come from public sources, interactions with exposed services, or earlier knowledge of the organization. The goal is to reduce uncertainty about who to target, which systems are reachable, and where a useful weakness might exist.

Typical activities

  • Identify public domains, internet-facing systems, technologies, and organizational relationships.
  • Research people, roles, suppliers, and public information that could support targeting or a convincing pretext.
  • Assess exposed services and potential weaknesses before choosing an entry route.

Example: A public job advertisement reveals a technology in use, while an exposed service confirms that it is reachable. Together, those observations can guide a later attempt; neither observation establishes that an intrusion occurred.

Passive research may leave no trace in the target’s own logs. Active scanning can produce network or application events, but legitimate monitoring can look similar. Discovery inside an already compromised environment serves a different purpose from pre-intrusion reconnaissance.

Defender’s question: What can an outsider learn about us, and which changes in exposure or scanning deserve investigation?

What to look for

Review exposed services and public information; distinguish external reconnaissance from discovery after access.

Use the model with care

The classic seven stages of Lockheed Martin’s Cyber Kill Chain are an organizing model, not a mandatory linear sequence. Stages can repeat, overlap, be skipped, or remain unobserved. Fileless, insider, identity, cloud, and AI attacks may not fit every stage. A reading association is not proof that an observed attack completed that stage.

MITRE ATT&CK describes adversary behaviors and tactical goals at a different level of detail. Its tactics are not interchangeable with these stages. The external technique links below are learning associations, not an official one-to-one mapping.

Start with CTI Kill Chain: An Analyst Guide With Real-World Evidence for case evidence and analytic limits, or the original framework reference (PDF).

Methods across stages

Use the CTI Analyst Field Manual for evidence and confidence, detection engineering guide for telemetry and validation, and practical learning paths for bounded exercises. These methods support several stages; they are not additional Kill Chain stages.

Source lifecycle, version boundaries, course access terms, and lab limitations still apply. Password recovery and lab exercises are learning analogues, not evidence of a real compromise.

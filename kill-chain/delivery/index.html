Understanding Delivery

Delivery is the point at which an adversary brings a malicious artifact or interaction to the target. It connects preparation to the environment the adversary wants to affect. The route can involve a message, a website, removable media, a trusted supplier, or a software distribution channel.

Typical activities

  • Send a malicious attachment or link through email or another communication channel.
  • Place adversary-controlled content where a target is expected to browse, download, or retrieve it.
  • Use removable media or a compromised software distribution path to bring an artifact into the environment.

Example: An employee receives an email with an attachment. Mail and download records can establish arrival, but receiving or saving the attachment does not establish that its payload executed.

Delivery and exploitation can be close together, as with browser-based attacks, but they answer different questions: how did it reach the target, and what caused unauthorized behavior? Some identity or insider incidents do not involve a conventional delivered malware file.

Defender’s question: Which route brought the artifact or interaction to the target, and can we connect that arrival to subsequent activity?

What to look for

Correlate email, browser, download, and endpoint evidence to establish how an artifact arrived.

Use the model with care

The classic seven stages of Lockheed Martin’s Cyber Kill Chain are an organizing model, not a mandatory linear sequence. Stages can repeat, overlap, be skipped, or remain unobserved. Fileless, insider, identity, cloud, and AI attacks may not fit every stage. A reading association is not proof that an observed attack completed that stage.

MITRE ATT&CK describes adversary behaviors and tactical goals at a different level of detail. Its tactics are not interchangeable with these stages. The external technique links below are learning associations, not an official one-to-one mapping.

Start with CTI Kill Chain: An Analyst Guide With Real-World Evidence for case evidence and analytic limits, or the original framework reference (PDF).

Methods across stages

Use the CTI Analyst Field Manual for evidence and confidence, detection engineering guide for telemetry and validation, and practical learning paths for bounded exercises. These methods support several stages; they are not additional Kill Chain stages.

Source lifecycle, version boundaries, course access terms, and lab limitations still apply. Password recovery and lab exercises are learning analogues, not evidence of a real compromise.

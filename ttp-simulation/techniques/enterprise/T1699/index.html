Complete technique description

Adversaries may input malicious prompts directly into an LLM’s user interface.[NIST Direct Prompt Injection] The prompts can be designed to bypass safeguards, manipulate context, override previous instructions, or coerce the LLM into unauthorized behavior.[OWASP Prompt Injection]

Adversaries may use direct prompt injection to Generate Content prohibited by the LLM's guardrails or to induce the system to utilize its capabilities in unintended or prohibited ways.[Gemini AI Misuse Jan 2025][Palo Alto Prompt Injection] Adversaries may achieve this by instructing the LLM to ignore previous instructions or safety rules, or by impersonating a role such as a researcher or privileged user.[Cybersec Guru Prompt Injection]

Source-linked mitigations
  • M1017 User Training

    Train users responsible for AI-enabled systems to recognize attempts to manipulate system instructions, role boundaries, or safety controls through user-provided prompts and to independently validate consequential AI-generated actions.

  • M1018 User Account Management

    Associate AI interactions with authenticated identities and constrain AI-mediated actions to the permissions of the initiating user. Do not allow prompt content to establish or elevate the authorization context used by downstream tools or services.

  • M1026 Privileged Account Management

    Restrict access to privileged AI capabilities and require explicit authorization or human approval for AI-generated actions involving administrative functions, sensitive resources, or privileged tools.

  • M1038 Execution Prevention

    Prevent user-supplied AI prompts from directly authorizing consequential actions. Enforce tool, API, command, and resource authorization outside the model and reject requested actions that exceed the permissions associated with the initiating user or agent task.

# Expanding the 1200km Cyber Knowledge Directory: New Source Assessments

## TL;DR
- **40 new sources are recommended** for addition across existing categories plus **three proposed new categories** (Supply Chain Security, OT/ICS Security, OSINT). The single highest-value additions are Google Project Zero, SANS Internet Storm Center, CISA ICS Advisories, capa, Eric Zimmerman Tools/KAPE, GreyNoise, SLSA, Sigstore, OpenSSF, VulnCheck KEV, and the vendor annual reports (Mandiant M-Trends, Microsoft Digital Defense Report, IBM X-Force Index, Red Canary Threat Detection Report).
- **Verify-before-recommend flags resolved:** capa is Mandiant/Google (FLARE); Triage is now Recorded Future; Snort is Cisco; KAPE is Kroll; Maltego is owned by Charlesbank; SpiderFoot is Intel 471. **MITRE Engage IS maintained** (V1 released Feb 28, 2022). **MITRE CAR is stale** (last analytics ~2021) and **Recon-ng is frozen** at v5.1.2 — both are rejected as "active" though noted as historically significant.
- **Two new tag-vocabulary terms** (`supply-chain-security`, `osint`) and **three new categories** are proposed; every offensive/dual-use resource carries the directory's standard caution line, and every freemium sandbox/enrichment service carries a registration/submission-exposure caveat.

## Key Findings

The existing 125-source directory has complete coverage of frameworks, DFIR tooling, CTI vendor labs, and AI security, but three practitioner domains are absent: **OT/ICS security**, **software supply-chain security**, and **OSINT**. These are the highest-value expansion areas. Within existing categories, the most defensible gaps are (a) crowd/vendor vulnerability enrichment (VulnCheck KEV, Project Zero, AttackerKB), (b) a small set of best-in-class free DFIR/malware tools (capa, EZ Tools/KAPE, Wazuh), (c) internet-scan and interactive-sandbox intelligence (GreyNoise, ANY.RUN, Triage), (d) the canonical dual-use offensive references (GTFOBins, LOLBAS, PayloadsAllTheThings, HackTricks), and (e) the major free annual threat reports (M-Trends, MDDR, X-Force, Red Canary).

Key verified facts: **VulnCheck KEV** — per VulnCheck's press release "VulnCheck KEV Surges to Track More than 3,600 Known Exploited Vulnerabilities": *"The volume of the VulnCheck KEV catalog exceeds the CISA KEV catalog by 173%. On average, the VulnCheck KEV is 27 days faster at informing users of known exploited vulnerabilities than the CISA KEV, and currently averages 125% more known exploited vulnerabilities added monthly."* Free community access requires registration. **Google Project Zero** — per its official disclosure policy, it operates a **90+30** model (90 days to fix, 30 days for patch adoption) with a 7-day deadline for in-the-wild bugs. **capa** — per github.com/mandiant/capa, it is *"The FLARE team's open-source tool to identify capabilities in executable files"*, Apache-2.0, "Copyright 2024 Google LLC." **MITRE Engage** V1 went live **February 28, 2022** (not 2023). **Recon-ng** is frozen at v5.1.2 — maintainer Tim Tomes (@lanmaster53) states on the project Wiki: *"Yeah... I stopped maintaining this when it got out of hand."*

### Vulnerability (existing category)

**VulnCheck KEV** — VulnCheck — https://vulncheck.com/kev — Category: Vulnerability; source type: Commercial Technical; access: **Freemium** (free Community, registration + attribution required); evidence use: Primary Operational; maintenance: **Continuous**; skill: beginner/intermediate/advanced. **Score 92 (A)** — authority 4.5, originality 4.5, maintenance 5, practical_value 4.7, transparency 4. Rationale: "Broadest evidence-cited known-exploited-vulnerability feed with earlier signal than CISA KEV; principal limitation: it is a vendor catalog requiring registration whose inclusion criteria and evidence links should be verified per CVE." Summary: VulnCheck KEV is a free community catalog of vulnerabilities reported exploited in the wild, fusing CISA KEV with additional exploit intelligence. It tracks over 3,600 KEVs — 173% more than CISA KEV — and on average lists exploitation 27 days earlier, with citations and links to proof-of-concept code in VulnCheck XDB. It is delivered as a dashboard, machine-readable JSON, and a free-key API. Because it is a vendor product with a registration requirement, treat it as a prioritization input to be cross-checked against CISA KEV, vendor advisories, and asset exposure. Strengths: earlier/broader exploitation signal; evidence citations per CVE; machine-readable API/JSON. Limitations: free tier requires registration and prominent attribution; a vendor-defined catalog whose evidence links can decay. Best-for: risk-based patch prioritization; enrichment of vulnerability pipelines; detection engineering off PoC references; early-warning triage. Related: cisa-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog, first-epss, national-vulnerability-database, exploit-database. **Freemium caveat:** registration required for API/community access.

**Google Project Zero** — Google — https://projectzero.google/ — Category: Vulnerability (or Threat Research); source type: Commercial Technical; access: Free; evidence use: Primary Authoritative; maintenance: Active. **Score 93 (A)** — authority 5, originality 5, maintenance 4.5, practical_value 4.4, transparency 4.5. Rationale: "Deep original 0-day root-cause research from a leading vulnerability team; principal limitation: coverage is highly selective and reflects the team's own targeting, not a comprehensive vulnerability view." Summary: Project Zero is Google's elite vulnerability-research team, publishing detailed root-cause analyses, its public issue tracker, and an annual "0-days in the wild" dataset. It operates a 90+30 disclosure policy (90 days to patch, 30 for adoption), with a 7-day deadline for actively exploited bugs. Practitioners use its write-ups to understand exploitation primitives, mitigations, and patch-gap dynamics. It does not provide operational IOCs or a comprehensive vulnerability catalog; treat its posts as authoritative technical case studies. Related: zero-day-initiative, cve-program, cisa-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog.

**Rapid7 AttackerKB** — Rapid7 — https://attackerkb.com/ — Category: Vulnerability; source type: Commercial Technical; access: Free (community; read-only API); evidence use: Secondary Corroborating / Mixed; maintenance: Active. **Score 85 (B)** — authority 3.5, originality 4, maintenance 4, practical_value 4.3, transparency 4. Rationale: "Crowd-sourced attacker-value assessments that add practitioner triage context to new CVEs; principal limitation: community opinions vary in rigor and are not a substitute for authoritative advisories." Summary: AttackerKB is Rapid7's community platform where researchers rate the "attacker value," exploitability, and shelf-life of emerging vulnerabilities, often with PoC references and attack-chain analysis. Use it to gauge community sentiment during triage, then corroborate with the vendor advisory, NVD, and CISA KEV. Related: cisa-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog, metasploit-documentation, exploit-database, zero-day-initiative.

### OT/ICS Security (PROPOSED NEW CATEGORY)

**CISA ICS Advisories** — CISA — https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories — source type: Government; access: Free; evidence use: Primary Authoritative; maintenance: Continuous. **Score 93 (A)** — authority 5, originality 4.5, maintenance 5, practical_value 4.5, transparency 5. Rationale: "Authoritative, continuously updated vulnerability advisories for ICS/OT/IoT products; principal limitation: advisories describe vendor-reported issues and require asset-specific validation before action in fragile OT environments." Summary: CISA's ICS Advisories are the U.S. government's continuously published bulletins on vulnerabilities in industrial control system, OT, and IoT products (Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, Mitsubishi, and dozens more), each with affected versions and mitigations. They are the closest thing to a canonical OT vulnerability feed. Because patching OT is operationally constrained, advisories should feed a risk-based, environment-validated process rather than blind remediation. Related: cisa-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog, mitre-att-and-ck (ICS), dragos.

**Dragos (WorldView / OT-CERT / Year in Review)** — Dragos, Inc. — https://www.dragos.com/resources — source type: Commercial Technical; access: **Freemium** (free OT-CERT membership and annual Year-in-Review; WorldView intel is paid); evidence use: Primary Operational; maintenance: Active/Periodic. **Score 90 (A)** — authority 4.5, originality 5, maintenance 4.5, practical_value 4.4, transparency 3.5. Rationale: "Leading OT/ICS threat-group and vulnerability research grounded in field incident response; principal limitation: telemetry and named threat-group clusters reflect Dragos's engagements and should be corroborated for attribution." Summary: Dragos publishes the field's most detailed OT/ICS threat research — annual OT/ICS Cybersecurity Year in Review, threat-group profiles (e.g., VOLTZITE, ELECTRUM, KAMACITE), and the free OT-CERT community program with resources and coordinated vulnerability disclosures. The 2026 report noted a 49% year-over-year rise in ransomware affecting industrial organizations. Treat quantitative and attribution claims as vendor telemetry to be cross-checked. **Freemium caveat:** OT-CERT and the Year-in-Review are free with registration; WorldView intelligence is a paid subscription. Related: cisa-ics-advisories, mitre-att-and-ck, the-dfir-report.

### Supply Chain Security (PROPOSED NEW CATEGORY)

**SLSA (Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts)** — OpenSSF (Linux Foundation) — https://slsa.dev/ — source type: Standards Body / Nonprofit Technical; access: Free; evidence use: Primary Authoritative; maintenance: Active. **Score 90 (A)** — authority 4.5, originality 4.5, maintenance 4.5, practical_value 4.4, transparency 5. Rationale: "Vendor-neutral, incrementally adoptable build-provenance framework now shipping by default in major CI ecosystems; principal limitation: SLSA verifies build integrity, not code safety, and spec versions have changed (v1.0 tracks → v1.2 Source track, Nov 2025)." Summary: SLSA is a Google-originated, OpenSSF-maintained framework defining progressive assurance levels for software build provenance, using in-toto attestations. GitHub Artifact Attestations and npm trusted publishing now emit SLSA provenance by default. Its own guidance stresses that a SLSA-compliant artifact can still be maliciously built from compromised source — provenance is not safety. Record the spec version you target. Related: sigstore, openssf, github-advisory-database, osv.

**Sigstore** — OpenSSF (Linux Foundation) — https://www.sigstore.dev/ — source type: Open Source Project / Nonprofit Technical; access: Free; evidence use: Primary Operational; maintenance: Active. **Score 90 (A)** — authority 4.5, originality 4.5, maintenance 5, practical_value 4.3, transparency 5. Rationale: "Free keyless artifact-signing infrastructure with a public transparency log, widely adopted across open-source ecosystems; principal limitation: verification at install time remains the least-adopted link, so signing alone does not guarantee downstream trust." Summary: Sigstore provides free tooling (cosign, Fulcio, Rekor) for signing and verifying software using ephemeral OIDC-tied keys logged to a public transparency ledger, removing long-term key management burden. It is the standard signing layer beneath SLSA provenance. Related: slsa, openssf, osv.

**OpenSSF (Scorecard / Best Practices)** — Open Source Security Foundation (Linux Foundation) — https://openssf.org/ — source type: Nonprofit Technical; access: Free; evidence use: Primary Authoritative; maintenance: Continuous. **Score 91 (A)** — authority 4.5, originality 4.5, maintenance 5, practical_value 4.5, transparency 5. Rationale: "Neutral coordination body publishing widely used supply-chain tools and guidance (Scorecard, Best Practices Badge, S2C2F, GUAC); principal limitation: it is a coordination and guidance layer whose tool scores are inputs, not guarantees of dependency safety." Summary: OpenSSF is the Linux Foundation's cross-industry body for open-source security, home to Scorecard (automated repo security health metrics), the Best Practices Badge, SLSA, Sigstore, and GUAC. DevSecOps teams use Scorecard to evaluate dependencies and the Best Practices guidance to harden projects. Related: slsa, sigstore, github-advisory-database, osv, semgrep.

### DFIR / Malware Analysis (existing categories)

**capa** — Mandiant / Google (FLARE team) — https://github.com/mandiant/capa — Category: Malware Analysis / Reverse Engineering; source type: Open Source Project; access: Free; evidence use: Primary Operational; maintenance: Active. **Score 92 (A)** — authority 4.5, originality 5, maintenance 5, practical_value 4.6, transparency 5. Rationale: "Open-source capability-detection tool with an ATT&CK/MBC-mapped rule set that accelerates triage and RE; principal limitation: capability inference is a hypothesis, not proof of malicious intent or execution." Summary: capa is the FLARE team's Apache-2.0 tool (Copyright 2024 Google LLC) that identifies capabilities in PE, ELF, .NET, shellcode, or sandbox reports and maps them to MITRE ATT&CK and the Malware Behavior Catalog. Analysts use it for triage, to guide reverse engineering (with the Ghidra/IDA integrations and capa Explorer Web), and to hunt across corpora. Capability hits are analytical hypotheses requiring analyst confirmation. Related: yara, ghidra, ida-free, malpedia, virustotal.

**Eric Zimmerman Tools / KAPE** — Eric Zimmerman / Kroll — https://ericzimmerman.github.io/ (KAPE via https://www.kroll.com/kape) — Category: DFIR; source type: Mixed License Tool (EZ Tools free/open; KAPE free for non-commercial, enterprise license for commercial); access: Free/Freemium; evidence use: Primary Operational; maintenance: Active. **Score 92 (A)** — authority 4.5, originality 5, maintenance 4.5, practical_value 4.8, transparency 4.5. Rationale: "De-facto standard free Windows artifact-parsing and triage-collection suite; principal limitation: parser output must be interpreted with knowledge of artifact provenance, and KAPE's commercial use requires an enterprise license." Summary: Eric Zimmerman's EZ Tools (MFTECmd, PECmd, EvtxECmd, RECmd, AmcacheParser, etc.) are free, portable Windows forensic parsers; KAPE (Kroll Artifact Parser and Extractor) is a high-speed triage collector/processor built on Targets and Modules. Together they form the backbone of modern Windows DFIR timelines. Results should be corroborated across artifact families and validated against known collection limits. **Note:** KAPE is free for individual/non-commercial use; commercial engagements require Kroll's enterprise license. Related: autopsy, the-sleuth-kit, plaso, timesketch, velociraptor.

**ANY.RUN** — ANY.RUN (Alexey Lapshin; HQ UAE) — https://any.run/ — Category: Malware Analysis; source type: Commercial Technical; access: **Freemium**; evidence use: Primary Operational; maintenance: Continuous. **Score 85 (B)** — authority 4, originality 4.5, maintenance 5, practical_value 4.4, transparency 3.5. Rationale: "Interactive cloud sandbox enabling real-time detonation and IOC extraction; principal limitation: free-tier submissions are public, exposing potentially sensitive samples, and verdicts require analyst validation." Summary: ANY.RUN is an interactive malware sandbox where analysts detonate files/URLs and manipulate the VM in real time across Windows/Linux/macOS/Android, extracting IOCs and ATT&CK mappings. The free community tier requires registration and makes analyses public. Use for behavioral triage and training; corroborate config/attribution with static analysis and other sources. **Freemium/privacy caveat:** free submissions are public — never submit sensitive or internal samples. **Dual-use caution:** May involve live malware, offensive techniques, or dual-use tooling; use only in an authorized isolated environment. Related: virustotal, malwarebazaar, joesandbox-equivalents, recorded-future-triage.

### OSINT (PROPOSED NEW CATEGORY)
